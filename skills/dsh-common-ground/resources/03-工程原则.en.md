# 03 · Engineering principles: the non-negotiables of data and structure

English | [中文](03-工程原则.md)

> This chapter corresponds to the **design layer** of 某工程 (`docs/01_工程原则_重建.md`) and governs "**what to do, why, and what must not be done**".
>
> **Why the three layers are not merged** (the logic in 某工程's own words): **the design definition must stay stable, while operational discipline follows the incidents** —
> the definition is the ruler for judging "does it work"; once the ruler drifts with the incidents, nobody can predict anything; operational discipline is the immediate patch after stepping into a pit, and the more it follows the incidents, the more accurate it gets.
> Merged, the two necessarily drag each other down. So the three documents use **division of labour ＋ pointers**, and conflicts are ruled by the priority chain (the design layer overrules the operational layer).
>
> **This chapter's bottom line**: **§8 Entity neutrality** governs every kind of "**judgement**" (permission judgement, visibility, UI show/hide, interface semantics),
> and **wherever the other clauses conflict with it, it prevails** — it is not "one more principle at the same level" (only **between layers** does the priority chain above apply).

---

## 1. Source-of-truth discipline (the bottom-most one)

**The rule**:
> **Anything derivable is never persisted; the smallest non-derivable fact must be persisted; one fact must not have two authoritative copies.**

**Why**: a derived column is a second fact, and two copies necessarily drift; and "record everything" lets **recording cost drag down information completeness**.
The original theme of 某工程 was eight characters — "**omit nothing, keep it concise and apt**": a single smooth entry path, while lookup and reporting are derived automatically by the archive.

**Four things that land it**:
1. **One concept has exactly one key space.** "Can log in" and "name only" are **flags on the row**, not two tables.
   ⇒ Any structure of the form "the uuid of some entity points at the synonymous row in another table" **is judged a violation; fix the structure first, then write code**.
2. **References store only the uuid; names are fetched live**: a reference does not copy the name and specification of the referenced object, and the display name is fetched live by the query.
3. **One record ＝ one fact**: stuffing several facts into one field is forbidden; free-text originals are kept, and structured fields only overlay them.
4. **The original text stays traceable**: when free text is ingested the original-text field is kept, and **no reductive abstraction is done**.

**What you look at**: whether the same fact is stored once in the UI, once in a report and once in the database; and whether changing one place makes you forget another.

**Citation**: `docs/01 §一`, `§二 P1/P4/P5/P6`, `§2.1`.

---

## 2. The iron rules of data safety

### 2.1 Persisted changes have exactly two routes

1. **The service interface**: judgement ＋ optimistic locking ＋ transaction ＋ automatic audit trail, all done in one pass.
2. **An approved one-off script**: back up first, read-only first, reversible.

**Hand-editing straight against the database is forbidden outright** — it bypasses validation, transactions and the audit trail, and is the source of orphan rows and "fake history".

### 2.2 With no foreign keys, write the guard yourself in full

The database layer of 某工程 **builds no foreign keys**, so "attached to the wrong project ／ self-reference ／ a cycle ／ deleting a parent row that still has children ／ a recipe using material outside its composition"
**is all caught by server-side guards**. The discipline for this kind of project:

- Writing a child row must, **in the same transaction**, first verify that the parent row exists and is not soft-deleted.
- **Writing a child row to a soft-deleted parent ⇒ an explicit refusal** (某工程 uses 410 GONE) — otherwise an orphan or an unrecoverable intermediate state appears.
- Every parent-child relation gets a self-check "if the parent is soft-deleted, the write is refused"; **when writing a new guard, first ask "was this row in the database when the validation ran"**.

### 2.3 Audit trail, concurrency, deletion

- **The change log is append-only**: guaranteed twice over by database-layer permissions ＋ triggers (no edit or delete entry point is provided).
- **Reversals are booked too**: a withdrawal writes a record pointing at the withdrawn sequence number; you may withdraw only your own most recent one; if the target has already moved on, it is refused.
- **Concurrency relies on version numbers**: the client carries a version number, a mismatch is a conflict (某工程 uses 409), and **nothing is ever auto-merged**.
- **Deletion is always a soft delete**: it goes to the recycle bin; no physical delete.
- **Several tiers of way back must be ready**: row-level withdrawal ／ soft-delete restore ／ an export archive ／ whole-database isolation.

### 2.4 Least privilege does not expand

**A new feature must not require widening the authorisation surface** (in particular, do not add delete permission to some table just to save trouble).
When the mechanism conflicts with authorisation, **change the mechanism to fit**, not the privilege.
An instance from 某工程: a certain table had no delete authorisation, so "cancelling a setting" was rewritten from "whole replacement" to "delta maintenance" —
**a write in the wrong direction is more dangerous than an error** (某工程 recorded "writing 'cancel' as an explicit refusal, the semantics exactly the opposite").

**Citation**: `docs/01 §3.1`, `§3.2`, `§3.3`, `§七`, `§6.8`.

---

## 3. Structural and field changes

### 3.1 Only grow; never rewrite old meanings

- An upgrade **only adds tables / columns / vocabularies**; rewriting a field's old meaning makes old data **silently change meaning**.
- A new column must not be **the same name with a different meaning** as an existing column.
- An upgrade must have the complete "five-piece set": migration script · compatible read window · reverse write-back · round-trip check · user confirmation.

### 3.2 Keep generated artifacts and decisions apart

> **Anything that is a "fact" is generated; anything that is "our decision" is hand-written.**

- Column name, column order, nullability, column type, comments ⇒ **generated** from the database metadata (hand-editing forbidden).
- Parent-row relations, cancellation semantics, the allowlist, numeric criteria ⇒ **hand-written** by a human.
- Two criteria nail it down: **the generated artifact must agree with the database**; **the generated artifact must go through the regular gates** (change the database and forget to regenerate ⇒ must go red).

### 3.3 Field definitions are fixed once and for all

Letting date and null definitions rest on "inference" bends the criteria (某工程, off by 8 hours in a timezone conversion, judged an unchanged row as "changed"):

- Date and time-of-day are separate columns, **stated column by column in the table comment**; only one string format is accepted.
- "Empty" is uniform across the whole database (某工程 standardises on NULL, and the entry point normalises the empty string).
- **The "cancellation" semantics of a child collection are defined by that table itself** (two-state / three-state differ from table to table), and **copying another table is forbidden**.

**Citation**: `docs/01 §六.1`–`§6.9`.

## 4. Permissions: narrowing layer by layer, deny wins, looked up live

### 4.1 The permission chain: judgement is a layer-by-layer narrowing chain (the number of layers is the project's decision; the invariants must all be present)

**The rule**: arrange "who can see what" into a **narrowing chain from "to whom" down to "to which piece of data"** —
**an earlier layer must not be widened by a later layer**. **The number of layers is the project's own decision** (see "where the layers come from" below);
**the six layers of 某工程 are only one instance, not a standard**.

**Eight invariants** (independent of the number of layers; miss one and it does not count as landed):

| # | Invariant | What a violation looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Single entry**: judgement goes through exactly one entry point (expansion in §4.2 item 3: hiding in the UI is not a line of defence) | A hand-written `if (role === 'admin')` in a route ／ in the UI ⇒ miss one place and it **silently fails** |
| 2 | **Narrowing layer by layer**: a later layer **must not widen** an earlier one | A fine-grained grant bypasses a coarse-grained gate ("item level" hands out a write permission the partition did not) |
| 3 | **Deny wins**: a denial at any layer is a denial | An **explicit denial** is stepped over by a finer layer ⇒ the tightening stops working |
| 4 | **Denials are locatable**: the receipt must say "**which layer refused**" | Knowing only "refused" ⇒ no re-check is possible, and no explanation can be given to the user |
| 5 | **A denial response does not leak existence** | Answering "forbidden" to a request that "should not know it exists" ⇒ it can be used to **enumerate** |
| 6 | **Missing context ≠ no judgement**: with no upper-level ownership, still run the layers that can run (expansion in §4.2 item 2) | "No project" skips permission judgement entirely ⇒ a read-only account **can edit and can delete** |
| 7 | **Visibility and listing share one source**: the judgement definition and the listing definition are **the same place** (expansion in §4.2 item 1) | Two definitions ⇒ "**exists but invisible**" records become detectable |
| 8 | **Derived rights are never persisted**: whoever executes may write ⇒ **computed by derivation**, not written into an allowlist | One change means clearing a whole swathe; drift is certain (the source-of-truth discipline of §1 applied to permissions) |

**Where the layers come from** (changing project means **walking through this again**; do not copy a layer count):

1. **List the dimensions** — write down every factor in your project that "**decides who can see what**". Typical ones:
   identity ／ role · tenant or partition · membership (project visibility) · feature switches · record state (soft-deleted ／ archived) ·
   record ownership · field sensitivity.
2. **Arrange them into a chain** — order them **from coarse to fine**: **to whom → where → may this feature be used → may this one be seen → may this field be seen**.
3. **One dimension may be owned by exactly one layer** — write down clearly where on the chain that layer sits.
4. **Three checks (hard requirements)**:
   - **Monotonicity**: is there any layer that can widen a layer **before** it?
   - **Dimension completeness**: is there **any dimension nobody owns**, or **one dimension judged once in each of two places**?
     (the **negative criterion**: ask each dimension by name "which layer owns you" — **cannot answer = a missing layer**; the same dimension appearing twice = **double judgement**)
   - **Denials are locatable**: for every kind of refusal, can you say **which layer** made it?

> **The number of layers is not the metric; a missed dimension is.** There may be fewer layers than six, or more; but **two layers must not be merged for looks** —
> merging means two dimensions lose their independent explanation, and invariants 2, 3 and 4 can no longer be verified.

**Example (某工程, 6 layers) — this is an example, not a standard**:

| Layer | Criterion (one sentence) |
|---|---|
| 1 Role | Administrators pass straight through; a read-only role only reads; writing needs an edit role |
| 2 Partition | Read ／ write flags per partition; public-class features **are not governed by this layer** |
| 3 Project visibility | The allowlist decides **whether the project can be seen** (the **only** visibility entry point) |
| 4 In-project features | By the **three states** of (project, account, feature key) (allowed ／ explicitly denied ／ inherit the upper layer); **deny wins** |
| 5 Record level | The project a record belongs to must be visible; soft-deleted records are visible only under "recycle bin" semantics |
| 6 Item-level grants | The task executor may by default read and write the task items it executes; documents it created itself may be read and written — **derived rights, not written into an allowlist** |

> **Even this layer count was questioned by that project itself**: layer 4 **stayed at 0 rows for a whole generation (almost nobody used it)**,
> and at the rewrite it was explicitly required to "**first ask whether it is needed at all — if not, do not build the table**" (`docs\存档\00_重构起点.md §2` item 2).
> ⇒ **"whether a given layer should exist" is itself the project's own decision** — which is exactly why this rule must be abstracted and cannot be copied.

**Citation**: `docs/01 §五` (the original text of the six-layer model and "narrowing layer by layer; an earlier layer must not be widened by a later one"), `§5.1`–`§5.3`;
`docs/00 §4.2` (judgement goes through the single entry point only); `docs/02 §三.5b` (the criterion for a 404 ／ 403 denial response: **will this refusal leak "existence"**);
`docs\存档\00_重构起点.md §2` ("should the layer exist" is a question that **must be answered afresh before starting work**).

### 4.2 Three hard disciplines

1. **Permission judgement is looked up live, never from a snapshot**: computing one set of permissions at login and using it ever after produces "after the privilege is revoked, this session can still see and still restore"
   (a measured defect in 某工程). **Visibility judgement must share one source with the listing** (judging "may this one be seen" with a second set of judgement can be used to enumerate the roster).
2. **"No project" is not "no constraints"**: records with no project context **still go through judgement** (the role and partition layers).
   Defect in 某工程: such records "skip permission judgement entirely" ⇒ a read-only account can edit and can delete.
3. **Hiding in the UI is only an aid, not a line of defence**: the rule must be enforced on the server; the UI only **presents the outcome**
   (capability declaration ＋ per-item permission judgement receipts ＋ a recognisable denial layer). Permission judgement happens only on the server, and **the front end is forbidden to derive permissions itself**.

### 4.3 A new read interface: first ask "who can see it"

An incident in 某工程: after an interface that could not be read was opened up, an old visibility hole was exposed at once — a non-administrator read **the whole database's** 5 rows of operation history,
and the detail carried the values before and after the change (`证据/S4_裁定落实.md`).
⇒ **Opening one read entry point ＝ opening a whole class of over-reach**; a new read interface must give the criterion for "who cannot see it" at the same time.

### 4.4 Permission judgement does not read entity features (**the §8 bottom line landing on permissions**)

**Entity neutrality is this chapter's bottom line** (see §8). **On the permission line it has exactly one landing: permission judgement looks only at responsibility inside the project, and does not read what the entity is.**

- **A permission path must contain no branch on category**: literals such as `kind === 'ai'`, "robot" and "human" — **not one of them is allowed**;
  and **the permission entry point's signature accepts no category field** — if it does not accept it, nothing inside the permission judgement can read it.
- **Capability differences do not enter permissions**: advantages in compute and data interaction go through **interface capability** (§8, four hard constraints, item 3), and **grant no extra permission** on that account;
  when a capability is absent, **generate no control** (the UI-side landing is [04 §4](04-界面契约.en.md) item 7), rather than generating a button that is refused when clicked.
- **The premise is "judgement has a single exit"**: hiding in the UI is not a line of defence, and permission judgement happens only on the server (§4.2 item 3) —
  when judgement is scattered across many places, "no place reads the category" cannot hold.

**Machine-checkable criteria** (already landed in 某工程; can be copied directly):

- Scan the files of **the permission and write paths**: no category-branching literals ＋ **negative-case controls**;
- **The permission entry point's signature accepts no category**.

**Why it is given its own place under permissions**: permissions are the **most frequent** place where "a feature is taken as a hard judgement" —
treating a certain kind of entity as a "special channel" or as a "restricted user" both disguise **a responsibility question as an identity question**.

## 5. Declarative single exit

**The rule**: **the registry declares; the executor executes**.

**Why**: rules scattered through execution code in many places **fail silently when one place is missed**.

**Landing it**: concentrate "writable columns / parent rows / cancellation semantics / current conditions / reference guards / derivation hooks / the delete allowlist" in **one registry** as declarations,
and the executor only executes. Two accompanying disciplines:

- **Every declaration must have an execution branch**, paired with a negative case; "a guard is registered but there is no execution branch" is **spinning in place**.
- **"May it be read" and "may it be written" are two different things**: for a new table, first ask "does the UI need to list it" (the high-risk defect in 某工程 was exactly "the four tables the UI needs to read
  were not registered as readable ⇒ the page became one whole error block, while every gate was green").

**Change one place, check every place**: before changing a shared component, count the copies — in 某工程 the front end measured "11 same-named functions across files",
and the whole UI component layer had been duplicated into two copies, so changing only one made the user re-verify twice with "no change".
⇒ **Within one runtime, multiple same-named, same-meaning implementations are all pending merge**; the criterion is "will the two evolve apart".
Only four kinds of duplication are allowed: different runtimes ／ **a criterion that must be implemented independently** (a criterion sharing the implementation under test ＝ same error, same pass) ／
fixtures and an independent recomputation ／ a one-line pure forwarding shell (it stops being valid the moment logic grows in it).

**Citation**: `docs/01 §3.3`, `§九`; `docs/26 §三`; `docs/00 §A.1`–`§A.5`.

## 6. Boundaries and priority

- **Cross-generation isolation**: the previous-generation project and the old database are **frozen read-only as a whole**; this generation neither reads nor writes the old database.
- **Quotations are always pointers (path ＋ section number), never wholesale transcription** — one transcription ＝ one extra copy ＝ certain drift.
- **A conclusion of the old case is not a conclusion of this project**: the old case is the previous generation's evidence; whether a given clause still holds today must be judged separately.
- **The priority chain** (on conflict): a ruling given by the user on the spot ＞ a ruling document ＞ the design layer ＞ the operational layer ＞ the index ＞ explanatory documents ＞ the historical archive.
- **Within one layer there is also a bottom line**: inside the design layer, **§8 entity neutrality is the bottom line** — where it conflicts with the other clauses, it prevails;
  only **between layers** does the priority chain above apply (the two are not the same thing; do not apply one to the other).
- **Number priority**: measurement ＞ a number written in a document; **do not write drift-prone numbers into documents** (the moment it is written down it starts expiring).
- **Unchecked ≠ carried over**: before an old clause has been checked through, **wherever there is a conflict the current ruling prevails**.

## 7. The four mistakes AI makes most easily in the design layer

| Failure mode | Recognition signal | Countermeasure |
|---|---|---|
| **Stale statement** | An authoritative document still carries the previous round's intermediate state (e.g. "a certain authority is still missing" when it has in fact been built) | Do not hard-code numbers that will change; correct stale sentences item by item in the revision record |
| **Transcription-type duplication** | The full body text of an old clause appears in a new file | Give only "a pointer ＋ a same/different mark" |
| **Changing the criterion to make the current state green** | A red light is explained away as "expected", and a structural problem is patched into a special case | When a criterion goes red, first ask "should the mechanism ／ the privilege be changed instead"; widening a definition must be listed separately with an audit trail and needs the user's confirmation |
| **Spinning in place where declaration and implementation disagree** | A guard ／ a capability is registered but has no entry point and no branch; the criterion pins a control that no longer exists | Pair every declaration with an execution branch and a criterion that can fail; shells must be marked explicitly (**registering a capability with no entry point ＝ an empty promise, worse than not writing it**) |

**Citation**: `docs/01 §十`, `§十一`, `§八`; `docs/29_全面检查报告.md`; `docs/00 §11.34`.

## 8. Entity neutrality (**the principle bottom line**: look at features before entering the project, and only at responsibility after)

> **This clause is a bottom line, not "a subsection inside some section"** (it used to hang below §4.4).
> What it governs is **a whole class of judgement** — **permission judgement, visibility, UI show/hide, interface semantics** — and it applies wherever there is "judgement";
> permissions are merely the **most frequent** of them. **Wherever the other clauses of this chapter conflict with it, it prevails**;
> 某工程 numbered it **principle nine**, treating it as **a bottom line above P1–P8**, and §8 of this chapter is **isomorphic** to it.
> **Its concrete landing in the permission direction** stays separately in §4.4 and is not repeated here.

**Definition (in the user's words)**:

> Entity neutrality is inspired by "**求同存异**". Different kinds of entity really do have different features, but for the project,
> **every participating entity is a "user"**, and **the purpose of joining the project should be the same**.
> ⇒ **A feature that existed before the project must not be used for hard judgement; it is fit only for advice and reference**;
> before **entering the project**, an entity should **choose a suitable joining identity** according to its features (taking on responsibility inside the project, and so on),
> and after entering, **move towards the same project purpose**.

> **Origin of the name**: the user's ruling text of 2026-09-22 reads — "某工程 should treat every using entity alike,
> and **the entities using 某工程 should collaborate on the principle of 求同存异**". The **English name `Common Ground`** of this series
> (skill slug `dsh-common-ground`; the description keeps the pinyin alias `qiutongcunyi` so it stays findable in Chinese) comes from exactly this sentence.

**Two layers of meaning; do not mix them**:

- **The collaboration layer ("difference" is put to positive use)**: the shape going forward is **several kinds of entity collaborating according to their own features** —
  features are used for **division of labour, working style and capability declaration** (who is suited to what, through which interface to collaborate), and **this is exactly the value of difference**.
- **The judgement layer ("sameness")**: **permission judgement, visibility, UI show/hide and interface semantics** inside the project
  **all look only at responsibility inside the project**, never at what it is. **For the permission-side mechanism and criteria see §4.4.**

**The dividing line is exactly "entering the project"**:

| | **Before** entering the project | **After** entering the project |
|---|---|---|
| What it looks at | The entity's own features (type, compute, interaction mode, available interfaces) | **Only what responsibility it carries inside the project** |
| What it is used for | **Choosing a joining identity**, settling division of labour and working style; usable for **advice** such as hints, recommendations and defaults | Permission judgement, visibility, UI show/hide, interface semantics |
| The standing of difference | Difference is here **digested and put to positive use** | **Treated alike** in **permission judgement, visibility and UI show/hide**; differences in interaction mode and compute are carried instead by the **interface capability** declaration (see item 3 below) |

**Four hard constraints** (the mechanism and the machine-checkable criteria for item 1 are in §4.4):

1. **Permission judgement looks only at responsibility inside the project, and reads no "feature that existed before the project"** — a permission path must contain no branch on category,
   and **the permission entry point's signature accepts no category field**. **For the mechanism and criteria see §4.4.**
2. **A feature may be "advice" but must not be a "criterion"**: it may serve as a hint, a recommendation, a default or an ordering reference;
   the moment it is used to **refuse or admit** an operation, this clause is already violated.
3. **Capability differences are written on "interface capability", not on "permissions"**: advantages in compute and data interaction are carried by **interface capabilities** such as
   **batch, incremental, subscription and idempotency** (**declared in exactly one place**), and **grant no extra permission**;
   **a missing capability ⇒ generate no control** (the UI-side landing is [04 §4](04-界面契约.en.md) item 7), rather than generating a button that is refused when clicked.
4. **Wording and guidance must not imply "a certain kind of entity cannot do something"**: UI copy drops the word "person" (execute ／ create ／ participate ／ take responsibility),
   and documents, comments and error copy say only "**what is to be done, and what responsibility it requires**".

**Machine-checkable criteria (this principle's side)**:

- **User-visible strings carry no residue of the old wording** (the mapping table for dropping the word "person"); permitted exceptions are **listed one by one in the allowlist with the reason written out**;
- For the two criteria on the permission side see §4.4.

**Corollary: projects may nest (derived projects).** An entity may carry **ruling responsibility** inside a **derived project** — **so may an AI**:
once a natural person has settled a requirement in the parent project, an AI may perfectly well start a derived project for that requirement and **collaborate with other AI entities** inside it.
Two hard constraints: **① serve the parent** (the purpose of a derived project is exactly to finish what the parent assigned); **② never contradict the parent**
(once it contradicts, or requires changing the parent's definition ⇒ **escalate to the parent's deciding party**; never change it yourself).
For the landing shape and the responsibility chain see [01 §1](01-协作的基本盘.en.md).

> **Source (three states)**: this corollary comes from the user's addition in the 2026 round — **the original text of 某工程 has no "derived project" clause**;
> it is an addition of this series and **has not been verified in the field**.

**Why it deserves a section of its own**: this clause directly decides "**can an AI participate in collaboration as a using entity**".
Treating an AI as a "special channel" or as a "restricted user" — both disguise **a responsibility question as an identity question** —
and this is exactly the typical shape of "a feature that existed before the project being taken as a hard judgement".

**The cost (stated honestly)**: the wording change has a very wide surface (it touches the vocabulary and the assertions), and **historical fields inside table names / column names** belong to the structural layer,
so they must be changed **as a separate step** (including DDL and the backfill definition), and **must not be changed casually along with the copy**.

**Three places this principle does not hard-code (left to the specific project)**:

1. **The concrete latitude of "advice and reference"**: the general definition is "usable for hints, recommendations, defaults and ordering; **never usable to refuse or admit**".
   As for **at which steps and in what way** features are used for advice, **the specific project decides** — this series does not prescribe it in advance.
2. **The mechanism for "choosing a joining identity"**: whether to set an explicit identity-choice step at the point of entering the project, and how to present the responsibility of each kind of identity,
   is **project-layer design**. This clause requires only that "**judgement after entering does not look back at features**"; it does not prescribe what to do before entering.
3. **"The purpose of joining should be the same"**: this is a **guiding idea**, not a decidable clause — logically, **only entities with the same purpose will take part in the same project**.
   It is therefore not used to set an admission judgement, nor to do any hard filtering on that basis.

**Where it does not apply (to prevent over-application)**: this clause governs **judgement** — **permission judgement, visibility, UI show/hide, interface semantics**.
**A title or note that "points at a reader" is not a violation**: for example "How the natural person gives instructions" and "A one-page checklist for the natural person" are saying
"**who it is written for**", not "**who has the say**"; the criterion is **whether it is allocating responsibility, or designating a reader**.
(The user's ruling in the 2026 round: these two titles are kept.)
