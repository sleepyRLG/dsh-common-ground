# 08 · How the natural person gives instructions

English | [中文](08-给自然人的下指令方式.md)

> This piece is written for the person who **does not read code but has to make the call**: how to say "what I want" as something an AI can execute and you can accept.
>
> The first seven pieces lean "engineering discipline"; this one is entirely about **the actions on the directing side**. The material likewise comes from 某工程 —
> there the user did not write code, yet with a few instructions turned the whole project around; and it was likewise a few vague instructions that once caused a whole slice of rework.

---

## 1. Your role

> **This section is about the actions of the "deciding seat", not about that seat being one only a natural person may sit in.**
> This piece assumes its reader is a natural person; but **a seat has nothing to do with entity category** — when an AI sits in that seat inside a **derived project**,
> the actions below **apply just the same** (see [01 §1](01-协作的基本盘.en.md)).

**You produce two things: direction (what is to be achieved) and criteria (how it counts as achieved). The executor produces the implementation (how to write it, how to verify it).**

- You need not design the implementation for the AI — that wastes the part it is best at, and it also blurs where responsibility lies.
- But **the decision "what to change" must come out of your mouth**. The executor inferring requirements on its own is the most expensive source of error in this kind of collaboration;
  an executor in 某工程 set itself a rule: **any new semantics goes into the ruling document, and it changes by the ruling, no longer by its own inference**.
- **If you are the parent project**: when an AI starts a derived project, you must give it a **mandate** (what to do, what not to do) and accept its **contradiction escalations**;
  **if you are the deciding party of a derived project (AI or natural person)**: the parent's ruling is the ceiling, and **you must not change the parent's semantics on your own**.

---

### If you want to remember only three sentences

You need not memorise the rest of this piece. **If you can say these three sentences, the AI is obliged to catch them**:

1. "**First give me 2-3 options and the cost of each; do not change code yet.**"
2. "**____ is the positive case; ____ must be eliminated; do not touch ____.**"
3. "**Give me the command and the output; what is unverified? what cannot be verified in this version?**"

The rest (terminology, ledger, breakpoints, gates) **is the AI's responsibility, not your threshold** — if it fails at that, it has violated the protocol,
not that you are not professional enough.

---

## 2. Making a request: give criteria, not conclusions

| Say it this way (conclusion) | Change to saying it this way (criterion) |
|---|---|
| "the interface is ugly / too cluttered" | "**the overview page is the positive case, do not touch it**; the task page should match its density" |
| "too much information to take in" | "**the task page should first serve review, and horizontal dragging must be eliminated**" |
| "this is wrong" | "**A should follow B, and right now it follows C**" (object + fact + expectation) |
| "use your judgement" | "I lean towards option A; **if B costs less, tell me first**" |

**Three concrete practices**:

1. **State clearly "what this thing is to achieve"** — it decides the AI's order of work (the development order goes by **importance to the project's purpose**,
   not by technical difficulty).
2. **Say one passage at a time, and state the positive and negative cases clearly**. The user of 某工程 did exactly this: one instruction explicitly said "which page is the positive case, do not touch it",
   "what must be eliminated", "which entry point should be withdrawn". **With a positive case, the AI knows where the boundary is.**
3. **Name "what may not be touched"** — files, data, old versions. 某工程 has a rule: when the user declares "this round does not touch certain data",
   **there must be no write to it** (including a "reversible" write).

---

## 3. While discussing: have the AI give options first, do not rush to watch it act

- **Ask for options, not code**: have the AI produce a **proposal** (options + consequences + its preference); you tick one or edit the wording.
  The process in 某工程 is **proposal → you rule → write it into a contract → then change code**.
- **While the discussion is unfinished, ask for the affected code to be frozen**. The benefit is direct: **firing continuously at a moving target** is the most expensive rework.
  Once, 某工程 explicitly declared "**until you have finished speaking on the positive/negative cases and the ruling, I will not touch the interface code**".
- **Ask it to read back**: register your words sentence by sentence as entries (the key wording / its understanding / the landing point and status),
  so that one look tells you whether it misheard; where they do not match, **fix it on the spot**.
- **For anything whose semantics are not settled, do not let it "throw in a fix along the way"** — a fix thrown in along the way usually has to be overturned the next round.

---

## 4. Acceptance: how to review "completed"

When you see "completed / passed / re-verified", run through these five questions:

1. **Is there a criterion?** — Ask for "**command + output**", not the three words "already verified".
2. **Where is the unverified part?** — It must list it of its own accord, especially the impression-type things that can **only be eyeballed**.
   If the environment cannot show the interface at all, yet it writes "re-verified", then that sentence is wrong.
3. **Is what it verified the path I walk?** — The most expensive defect in 某工程 lives exactly here: every assertion in every place is right,
   **and the user path that joins them all up is walked by nobody** (the most typical kind: the account was created, the permissions were set,
   **and when I log in I can see nothing**).
4. **Has the number of passing items gone down? How many items are skipped, and why?**
5. **Has the criterion been changed?** — If it has, ask one question: "was only the letter loosened, or **was the nature loosened along with it**?"

**Plus one question you must ask**:

> **"What in this version still cannot be verified? Tell me first, and do not report it as a defect."**

某工程 gave a re-verification list every round, and explicitly marked "what this version cannot yet re-verify",
so that you would not spend time reporting things that "were never done in the first place" — **you have no time, and the AI should waste your time even less**.

---

## 5. When you find a problem: state the symptom, not your guess

**An effective defect report = how you got there + what you saw + what you expected.**

某工程 has a very practical lesson: **when you cannot reproduce the error the user reported, do not keep adding guesses**; give three things directly —
**the complete error (with status code) + the exact operation path + the input at the time**.

Two more:

- **One at a time**, each with a criterion. Reported all jumbled together, the AI will pick the easy one to do.
- **Keep the wording consistent**. If you mean A by a word and it understands B, it will write things that are "a little off everywhere" and **raise no error**.
  某工程 set up a glossary specifically for this, and ruled that **a terminology conflict comes back and asks you first**.
  So: when you change wording, say in passing "x here means y".

---

## 6. Three rules you yourself must keep

1. **State the project's purpose.** The AI will keep pushing "foundation-level but inconspicuous" features to the back
   (the original words of 某工程: foundation-level features are invisible in the interface, and are the easiest to be jumped by "along the way / conspicuous / was already being done last round").
   You need only say one sentence, "it is a foundational feature needed to achieve the purpose", and the order changes at once.
2. **In the unsettled period, be decisive once the direction is clear.** The cost of hanging long-term is that **later comers keep writing code on the wrong names / structures**.
   But "decisive" has three preconditions (have it give them to you item by item): **it can state which project purpose this supports - the scope of change has been measured and made clear - there is a criterion nailing down the result**;
   miss one and it is still "to be settled".
3. **Do not push with "hurry up".** In this kind of collaboration the most expensive thing is not slowness, it is **fast and not collected** (half-finished products, fake passes, a missing breakpoint).
   The original words of 某工程 are: "**stop first, then locate** — do not keep stacking changes in the mindset of 'it looks like it is almost done'."

---

## 7. When to call a halt

| Signal | What you should say |
|---|---|
| It says "about done", but cannot give a criterion | "Give the criterion first, then talk about completion." |
| It starts explaining the failure ("as expected") | "Judge first: **is the assertion wrong, or the code wrong**?" |
| Halfway through the change the direction changed | "**Roll back to a marked point** and then talk." — rolling back mark by mark costs very little; forcing it back is two layers of hidden trouble |
| It reports "what this version cannot yet verify" as a defect | "This version cannot verify it yet, note it for now, do not take up my time." |

The discipline of 某工程 can be copied directly: **a red light means roll back, and a red light must not be banked as one increment**; **rollbacks may happen, a breakpoint must not go missing**.

---

## 8. Five sentences you can copy directly

1. **Stating a requirement**: "What this thing is to achieve is ____. **First give me 2-3 options and the cost of each; do not change code yet.**"
2. **Giving a criterion**: "____ is the positive case, follow it; ____ must be eliminated. **Do not touch ____.**"
3. **Asking for a ruling**: "Organise the passage above into entries for me to confirm, **mark the places that are not settled**; do not touch this part before I nod."
4. **Reviewing a pass**: "Give me the **command and the output**. **What is unverified? What cannot be verified in this version?**"
5. **Reporting a defect**: "I did ____, saw ____, expected ____. (**the complete error and path attached**)"
