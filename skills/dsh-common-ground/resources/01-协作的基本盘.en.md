# 01 · Collaboration basics: who directs, how to discuss, how to rule

English | [中文](01-协作的基本盘.md)

> This chapter is about **the rules between a natural person and an AI**; it covers neither code nor UI.
> The experience of 某工程 is: **technical incidents can mostly be blocked by tools and criteria, and almost every truly expensive loss comes out of "who has the last word"** —
> an AI changing requirements on its own inference, acting before the semantics are settled, words said that land nowhere, and the goal already unfindable when the next person takes over.
>
> The format of every item: **rule → why (the cost) → how to do it → citation**.
> The "what you look at" column is for the natural person: without reading code, you can still use it to judge whether the AI keeps to the rules.

---

## 1. Direction is ruled by the **project's deciding party**; the executor may not change requirements on its own inference

> **Who is "the deciding party"?** — **the entity that carries ruling responsibility in the project, whatever it is**.
> Usually a natural person, but **not necessarily**: an AI can also carry ruling responsibility in a **derived project** (see the end of this section).
> Pinning ruling authority **onto the category "natural person"** is exactly the wording [03 §8](03-工程原则.en.md) forbids.

**Rule**: adding or changing semantics always goes **proposal first, wait for the ruling, then act**. The executor must not change the requirement itself along the way just because something "looks more reasonable".

**Why**: 某工程 has an explicit record of self-restraint — after the UI semantics were settled, the executor's own words were
"**every new piece of semantics goes into `裁定案.md`; I change according to the ruling and no longer change the UI on my own inference**".
On the other side of that sentence lies a real history of rework: the executor had changed the specification table on an "assumed" model, while the project **already had** the real
attribute-definition table; the two coexisting misaligned the direction of work in three places and broke saving for three versions in a row (`docs/00 §A.9`).

**How to do it**:
- **Executor**: when you hit "the requirement does not say, but I think it should be like this", **stop and ask one question**, and lay out the candidate options and their costs (the proposal regime).
- **Executor**: for changes already made that may contradict the direction, **register them honestly and give the rollback point**; do not keep them quietly.
- **Deciding party**: it is enough to say clearly "what this is meant to accomplish"; you need not design the implementation yourself — but **every decision on "what to change" must come out of your mouth**.
  — **whichever entity sits in this seat, this clause applies, whether it is a natural person or an AI.**

**What you look at**: whether the executor first said "what you want is X, right?" before changing anything; whether every change can be traced to **something the deciding party said**.

> **One corollary (entity neutrality)**: in this way of collaborating, **an AI is a "using entity / user" just like a natural person** —
> judgement looks only at **what responsibility it carries** in the project, never at **what it is**; and "features that existed before the project"
> **serve collaboration and division of labour as advice only**, and never as a criterion. See [03 §8](03-工程原则.en.md) for detail.

### Derived project: an AI may also be the deciding party, but must not contradict the parent

**The shape**: after a natural person settles a requirement in a **parent project**, **an AI may perfectly well start a derived project for that requirement**
(for example "turn this protocol into a skill") and **collaborate with other AI entities** inside the derived project;
at that point **the deciding party of the derived project is that AI**, not a natural person — this is the positive case of entity neutrality, not an exception.

**Two hard constraints**:

1. **Serve the parent**: the purpose of the derived project's existence is **to finish this thing the parent assigned**, not to set up a goal of its own.
2. **Never contradict**: the derived project's semantics, outputs and practices **must not contradict the parent project's rulings**;
   once they contradict, or a change to the parent's semantics turns out to be needed ⇒ **stop and escalate to the parent's deciding party** — **you may not change the parent's requirement yourself**.

**The responsibility chain**: a derived project **may have its own authority**, but its **ceiling is the parent project's ruling** (for the chain see [§4](01-协作的基本盘.en.md)).

**Landing it in writing**: the first thing a derived project does at kick-off is **write down "who the parent project is, how far the mandate reaches, and what to do on a contradiction"** —
otherwise it is an island where "nobody knows whose word to take".

**Citation**: `docs\13_现状一页纸.md §九.4`; `裁定案.md` (the ruling layer);
`docs\00_开发原则_沿用.md §A.9`.
**The "derived project" section** comes from the user's addition in the 2026 round — **the original text of 某工程 has no such clause**; it is an addition of this series (**not verified in the field**).

## 2. The three states of a ruling: proposed / ruled / not yet ruled

**Rule**: every pending item must sit in one of the three states and **be written in a file**; anything "not yet ruled" **must not be used as a basis** for acting.

**Why**: without the three states, two typical incidents appear:
① using a **proposal** as **current semantics** (某工程's criterion is blunt: **does the file header say "finalised / landed / pending a ruling" —
anything "pending a ruling" cannot be used as a basis**);
② treating "I mentioned it" as "you agreed", and then going on doing it wrong all the way.

**How to do it**:
- In the ruling document, collect them in one fixed section: **the user's own words (the ruling text) + the execution status**. Avoid paraphrase — paraphrase drifts.
- Items pending a ruling are **gathered into exactly one place** (they must not be scattered across documents), or they go missing (某工程 once had "four pending rulings missing and unsummarised").
- Before wrapping up each round, scan once: is there anything already ruled but not landed, or landed but not closed out?

**What you look at**: whether the AI's report "N items are still waiting for your nod" points at the same single list; whether the same matter waits for you in only **one** place.

**Citation**: `裁定案.md §七` (the ruling record + the execution status); `文档索引.md §五.4`;
`docs\13_现状一页纸.md §九.4`.

## 3. Discussion and action are separated: if the semantics are not settled, do not touch the code involved

**Rule**: while a matter is still under discussion (you are giving positive cases, negative cases, examples, changing your mind), **the code involved is frozen**;
wait until the rulings are all in, then act in one pass.

**Why**: 某工程 once handled this in a very typical way: the user issued a "first major instruction" about the UI and had only said the first paragraph,
and what the executor did was — register the instruction sentence by sentence as ledger entries I1–I8, write six items of exchange of views, and then announce
"**the UI code is frozen: I will not touch the UI until you have finished on the positive and negative cases and the rulings**".
What that blocked is the most expensive kind of rework: **firing continuously at a moving target**.

**How to do it**:
- During discussion the AI does only three things — **register the ledger, offer views in exchange, assess the cost** — and changes no code.
- "The three things already clear" that come up during discussion may land first (e.g. "the overview page is the positive case, do not touch it"); the rest waits until all are in.
- For changes already made whose direction may still change: **mark them "one-click rollback"** and give the rollback point (tag / commit).

**What you look at**: after you finish a stretch of thinking, does the AI go and change things first, or does it first turn your words into entries and point out what is not settled in them?

**Citation**: `docs\13_现状一页纸.md §三`, `§四`; `docs\12_交互界面重定义_讨论准备.md §七／§八`.

## 4. Whose word wins on a conflict: the priority chain

**Rule**: when two things say different things, judge by a fixed chain; **taking whatever suits you on the spot is forbidden**.

The chain in force in 某工程 (`docs/01 §十一`, `文档索引.md §四`):

```
A ruling given on the spot  >  the ruling document  >  the design layer (engineering principles)  >  the operational layer (development principles)
             >  the document index  >  the explanatory document (README)  >  the historical archive
```

**Inside a derived project one more layer sits on top of the chain** (see [§1](01-协作的基本盘.en.md)):

```
The parent project's ruling
   >  [ a ruling inside the derived project  >  its design layer  >  the operational layer  >  the index  >  the explanatory document  >  the historical archive ]
```

**A derived project may have its own authority, but the ceiling is the parent project's ruling**; any contradiction **escalates to the parent's deciding party**,
and **the parent's semantics may not be changed on your own**.

**There are also three "number priorities"**:
1. **The actual files and the live state in the database > any number written in any document** (`information_schema` / `SHOW COLUMNS` / `SELECT`, measured now).
2. **For authorisation, only `SHOW GRANTS FOR CURRENT_USER()` counts** — a low-privilege identity reading metadata gets a false "nothing there".
3. **Unchecked ≠ carried over**: an old clause that has not been checked item by item does not count as current semantics.

**Why**: with no chain, an AI tends to cite "the one easiest to cite", and the historical archive and proposals happen to be the easiest to cite wrongly.
某工程 wrote "numbers drift, so never hard-code a number; when you need a number, measure it now" straight up as a rule (`docs/00 §11.34` side note, `文档索引.md §四`).

**How to do it**: write this chain **in the most prominent place in the documentation**, and require: when citing the historical archive you **must explicitly say "this is historical semantics"**.

**What you look at**: when the AI cites a rule, does it say which layer that rule comes from?

**Citation**: `docs\01_工程原则_重建.md §十一`; `文档索引.md §四`.

## 5. Align terminology first, then discuss requirements

**Rule**: **one word is allowed one meaning only**; when the user and the AI understand the same word differently, **tell the user first, then act**.

**Why**: 某工程 made terminology alignment a separate document and laid down "tell the user first" for a conflict of words. The reason is that when the same word
(for example "module", "phase", "responsible") means different things in the user's mouth and in the data model, the code written comes out **slightly off everywhere**,
and this kind of error **does not report itself** — it shows up only when the user re-checks and says "that is not what I meant" (某工程 recorded: a change of wording pulls at assertions and at the structural layer,
so it must be a step of its own, and must not be done along the way together with a copy change).

**How to do it**:
- Keep a **glossary** (the one spelling + which spellings not to use), and designate a **single landing file** (copy must not be duplicated inline in several places).
- On finding an ambiguity: **ask first** "your X means A or B", then decide where it lands.
- Renaming must be **graded**: the wording of UI copy and the table and column names of the data layer belong to different change layers and must not be lumped into one cut.

**What you look at**: whether the words the AI uses when restating your requirement **are the same ones** you said.

**Citation**: `docs\31_词汇表_术语对齐.md`; `文档索引.md §一`, `§五.4`;
old case `交互策略.md §6`.

## 6. The ledger: to-dos, pending rulings and breakpoints each have one place

**Rule**: for everything **unfinished / half-done / pending a ruling**, a line appears in the fixed ledger in the same round (id · status · landing point ·
recovery steps · basis). **A rollback may happen; a breakpoint must not go missing.**

**Why**: within a single day 某工程 had several "rolled back halfway" incidents (criteria not synced, a slice cut wrong, an anchor containing a space).
The rollback itself was right (a red light is never banked), **but if the breakpoint is not written into the ledger, the next round has to survey it all over again** — time is burned twice over.
Another related lesson is "**a lost goal**": a goal scheduled in the plan long before dropped out of view after round-by-round handovers,
and was found only later — which is why they set the "index" up as the first line of defence against losing things (see [07](07-文档体系与长期接手.en.md)).

**How to do it**:
- Build a **breakpoint ledger**: id, status, landing point (file/line/commit), recovery steps, basis.
- Build a **pending-ruling summary** (this one place only).
- Discipline: **anything you "plan to leave to the next round" must appear as a line in the ledger in the same round**; avoid manufacturing unnecessary breakpoints —
  finish a thing in one pass (code change + criteria synced + gates green + recorded), and never leave half of it.

**What you look at**: when the AI says "let us leave this for now", does it also give you a line that can be indexed and recovered from?

**Citation**: `docs\00_开发原则_沿用.md §A.7`; `docs\25_断点台账.md`;
the file header of `文档索引.md`.

## 7. Report a stretch of work with "three states + pointers", not a conclusion

**Rule**: every time the AI reports, it gives at least four things: **what was changed / why it was changed that way / the criteria (command + output) / the part it could not verify**.
A conclusion must be traceable back; **"should be fine" may not stand in for a criterion**.

**Why**: 某工程 wrote this into a deliverable checklist (`docs/00 §7`): one change must leave behind at least an evidence file,
self-check output, demo material and a documentation registration; and when writing evidence there are three hard requirements — **a conclusion must carry a re-checkable criterion (command + output)**,
**the failure and the correction process must be kept** (keep only the successful version and the next round steps into the same pit again),
and **a semantics change must be listed separately** (if any criterion or allowlist was loosened for a fix, that must be stated explicitly).

**How to do it**:
- Reporting template: `verified (command + output) | unverified | known-unsolved`; all three columns may be empty, but they must not be mixed.
- "I cannot run it" ≠ "it need not be run": if you cannot run it, **mark the boundary honestly** (for example no browser ⇒ the look of the UI counts as unverified),
  and **it must not be written as "re-verified"**.
- The demo-data stage and the real-data stage have **different operating scales**: use "has real data been entered yet" as the switch;
  after the switch there must be a backup, a `--check` first, rollback possible, and item-by-item re-checking.

**What you look at**: when the AI reports "passed", does it give you the command and the output at the same time; does it explicitly list "what this version still cannot verify — do not report it as a defect".

**Citation**: `docs\00_开发原则_沿用.md §7`, `§A.8`, `§11.15`;
`docs\01_工程原则_重建.md §九.2`, `§九.3`.

## 8. The four things on the deciding party's side

> For a fuller picture of the "directing side's actions" see [08 · How the natural person gives instructions](08-给自然人的下指令方式.en.md).
> **A "deciding party" is a seat, not an identity** — usually a natural person sits in it, and an AI may sit in it inside a derived project (see [§1](01-协作的基本盘.en.md));
> the four items below **follow the seat**.

However many rules the AI has, they cannot block vagueness on the directing side. In 某工程's practice, the party carrying ruling responsibility played four irreplaceable roles
(in 某工程 that seat was held by a natural person):

| What you do | Why | How 某工程 worded it |
|---|---|---|
| **Give criteria, not conclusions** | Words like "the overview page is the positive case, do not touch it" and "review on the main line comes first" are far more executable than "the UI does not look good" | The user's ruling texts round by round go into the ruling document (`裁定案.md`) |
| **Say clearly "what this project is for"** | The development order is set by **how important a thing is to the project's purpose**, not by technical difficulty | `docs/00 §11.35` (a rule the user laid down) |
| **While things are unformed, be decisive once the direction is clear** | The cost of suspension is that whoever comes later keeps writing code on the wrong names / structures | `docs/00 §11.36` (a rule the user laid down: grasp = being able to say which purpose it supports + the change surface measured and clear + a criterion nailing down the result) |
| **Name "what must not be touched"** | Example: during re-verification, do not touch certain data (including writes that are "reversible") | `docs/00 §6.1` |

## 9. Multiple writers and multiple sessions: who writes, where, and who is the source of truth

**Premise**: a project often has **more than one writer** — several AI sessions, several models, several natural persons, possibly working on the same codebase and the same table at the same time.
某工程's multiple-writer incidents all have the same shape: **every writer believed it was the only writer**.

1. **One source of truth, one writer at a time.** Before acting, declare "which files / which tables I am going to change" (the **write scope**).
2. **Write scopes should not intersect as far as possible.** Divide by file, directory and table; when overlap is unavoidable, **serialise** — wait for the other side to finish and hand over,
   and do not "each change half and then merge".
3. **Before changing the same file, first check "process = disk".** What you are looking at may not be the current version, and it may be something someone else just changed
   (某工程's most frequent misjudgement is "verifying on a stale process").
4. **Hand over a receipt at the end of every session**: which files were changed / what the criteria produced / which breakpoints remain. No receipt equals **a missing writer**,
   and the next person taking over has to survey it all again.
5. **Deletes and bulk operations follow the exact target only.** Temporary data in use by concurrent work is what gets deleted by mistake
   (a real case in 某工程: a cleanup script deleted by prefix, wiping out a temporary project **that a concurrent job was running**, and it could not be rolled back).
6. **When two executors reach opposite conclusions, do not vote and do not each change one spot**: go back to **the probe** — build the smallest experiment that can distinguish the two explanations and settle it with that;
   if it is a disagreement over semantics, escalate it to a **ruling**.
   (a real case in 某工程: two executors reached opposite conclusions on "will this row land in the database once written", while **the comment on disk stood on the wrong side**.)
7. **Who is the source of truth must be written down hard**: every document and every piece of code has exactly one authority; on a conflict of semantics, **the ruling document** prevails (see §4, the priority chain).
8. **The right to review is not left to whoever wrote the criterion**: a criterion must be handed over together with **the evidence, the boundary and the re-run command**, and re-checked by **another entity**;
   if the writer and the reviewer are the same, "passed" is only self-confirmation (see [05 §3](05-验证与假通过.en.md), a criterion's own three self-proof obligations).

> **Source (three states)**: items 1–5 and 7 come from real multiple-writer incidents in 某工程; item 6 generalises its **probe discipline**
> to the multiple-writer setting (the original text speaks of "attributing by probe" and does not address multiple writers directly), and is **not verified in the field in this shape**.

**Citation**: `docs/00 §11.13`, `§11.15`, `§11.18`, `§4.2`; `证据\S4.5_断点与恢复.md`.

## 10. A one-page checklist (can be copied straight into the project's conventions)

- [ ] There is a priority chain, written in a prominent place.
- [ ] There is a **ruling record** (the user's own words + the execution status), and "pending a ruling" has **exactly one summary place**.
- [ ] There is a **breakpoint ledger**, with the rule "register one line in the same round".
- [ ] There is a **glossary**, laying down "on a conflict, tell the user first".
- [ ] The reporting format = `verified / unverified / known-unsolved`, and **a conclusion must carry the command and the output**.
- [ ] Agreed: the AI **does not change requirements by inference**; during discussion **the code involved is frozen**.
- [ ] Agreed: anything "left to the next round" **is recorded in the same round**, with the landing point and the recovery steps written down.
- [ ] Multiple writers: **declare the write scope** before acting; on overlapping scopes **serialise**; **hand over a receipt** at the end of every session (see §9).
